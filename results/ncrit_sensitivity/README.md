# SG6043 `n_crit` sensitivity study — results

PROJECT_PLAN.md §1.2. Generated by `python -m validation.ncrit_sensitivity`
(from `src/`), 2026-09-09. Compares XFOIL against the UIUC LSAT Vol. 3
clean-model measurements (`data/sg6043_uiuc_lsat/`) at Re = 100k, 150k, 200k,
300k, 400k, 500k, for `n_crit ∈ {5, 7, 9, 11, 13}`, at the alpha range
(−8…18°) and panel count (240) `config/polars_sg6043.yaml` specifies for the
real cache build.

## Files in this directory

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `ncrit_sensitivity_metrics.csv` | Every `(Re, n_crit)` pair: converged-point count, Cl/Cd RMSE vs UIUC (and how many measured points the comparison used), Cl_max and Cd_min from XFOIL. |
| `ncrit_sensitivity_summary.json` | The script's own aggregate (mean RMSE per `n_crit` across the six Re) and its auto-pick. **Superseded by the per-Reynolds analysis below** — see *Why 9, not the script's auto-pick of 11*. |
| `sg6043_ncrit_sweep_Re<N>.png` | Six plots (one per Re), Cl and Cd vs alpha, all five `n_crit` curves against the UIUC points. |
| `sg6043_ncrit_rmse_summary.png` | Cl RMSE vs `n_crit`, one line per Re. |

Raw XFOIL output for every run (converged and timed-out) is at
`results/_archive/ncrit_sensitivity/` — not committed (regenerable scratch,
see `.gitignore`), but present locally if a specific run needs re-inspecting.

## Why 9, not the script's auto-pick of 11

`ncrit_sensitivity_summary.json` picked 11, by averaging Cl RMSE and Cd RMSE
*separately* across the six Re and combining the two means
(`mean_cl_rmse + 10·mean_cd_rmse`: 9 → 0.1261, 11 → 0.1259). That is correct
arithmetic but the wrong question — it can call a value "better on average"
even if it never wins in a single individual comparison, because the mean
blurs across the Reynolds numbers where the two candidates actually compete.

Recomputing the same combined score **per Re before comparing**, on the two
candidates with complete six-Re coverage:

| Re | n_crit=9 score | n_crit=11 score | winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100k | 0.2272 | 0.2909 | **9** |
| 150k | 0.1951 | 0.1137 | 11 |
| 200k | 0.1332 | 0.1456 | **9** |
| 300k | 0.1171 | 0.1257 | **9** |
| 400k | 0.0361 | 0.0376 | **9** |
| 500k | 0.0476 | 0.0421 | 11 |

`n_crit = 9` wins at 100k, 200k, 300k and 400k — the band that overlaps the
design rotor's actual operating range (plan §3.3: ~150k–450k at the rated
condition) and sits closest to the unmeasured 40k–100k floor the real cache has
to cover down to. `n_crit = 11` wins at 150k and 500k. The mean-of-means
aggregate let those two wins (one of them by a wide margin, 150k) outweigh four
narrower losses.

Three further candidates are **incompletely characterised** rather than
losing, and are not ruled out: 5, 7 and 13 are each missing one Reynolds
number (5 at 300k, 7 at 150k, 13 at 200k), and 13 produced the single best Cl
score at 300k (0.0410 against 9's 0.0978). The choice of 9 is a calibration on
the Reynolds-number band the rotor uses, not a fitted optimum.

Two independent checks support 9 over 11 rather than just the score:

- **Physical plausibility.** `n_crit = 9` is XFOIL's own default, representing
  a clean low-turbulence wind tunnel — which is exactly what the UIUC LSAT
  facility is. Matching the *measurement environment's* transition behaviour
  when validating against tunnel data is the theoretically expected choice.
  (This is the opposite reasoning from S809's `Ncrit = 5`: that cache is
  matched to *atmospheric* turbulence for a thick, stall-prone section, not
  to the tunnel it was measured in — see the 2026-07-26 journal entry. The
  two calibrations are not supposed to agree, and they don't.)
- **Re = 300k free-transition sanity check** (plan §1.2's own flag). XFOIL's
  Cl_max at `n_crit=9`/Re=300k is 1.6456 against the UIUC lift curve's 1.654
  — 0.008 off. `n_crit=11` gives 1.6846, 0.031 off. 9 passes this check
  tighter.

## What this study did not settle

**`n_crit = 5, 7, 13` are not ruled out — they are incompletely characterized,
not disqualified on merit.** Each hit the 400 s per-run timeout at exactly one
Reynolds number (5 at 300k, 7 at 150k, 13 at 200k). The partial XFOIL output
for all three (`results/_archive/ncrit_sensitivity/*_ncrit{5,7,13}.txt`, not
committed) shows the solver was still stepping through the deep-negative-alpha
end of the sweep, not stuck — a longer timeout would likely have finished
them. A targeted re-run of just those three at 1200 s was started but stopped
before completion (session time budget), so this remains open.

This matters because `n_crit = 13` produced the single best score anywhere in
the table at Re=300k (0.0410, roughly half of 9's own 300k score) — a strong
local result that might or might not generalise; it cannot be weighed against
9 or 11 without its missing 200k point. **If this calibration is ever
revisited** (e.g. Task 3's interpolant fit exposes a problem traceable to
transition modelling), re-running those three specific `(Re, n_crit)` pairs
at a longer timeout is the first thing to do before concluding 9 remains best.

**Nothing below Re = 100k was tested here** — the UIUC data doesn't reach
lower, and this study can't validate what isn't measured. The design
envelope's floor is 49k (Task 2). `n_crit = 9`'s validity down to there is an
assumption, not a result — named as `low_re_caveat` in
`config/polars_sg6043.yaml` and belongs in the limitations chapter (plan
§3.3, risk R4), not treated as resolved by this study.
