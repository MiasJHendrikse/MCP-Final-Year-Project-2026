# SG6043 polar table (design airfoil)

XFOIL polar cache for the SG6043, the design rotor's airfoil
(see `data/airfoils/README.md` for the coordinate provenance). One
CSV per Reynolds number, columns `alpha,cl,cd,cm,source`, alpha −180° to
+180° on a 0.5° grid — XFOIL-converged from −8° to +18°, Viterna
extrapolation outside that (see *File format* below). Re = 40k–1.0M in 13
steps (40k, 60k, 80k, 100k, 150k, 200k, 300k, 400k, 500k, 600k, 700k, 800k,
1.0M) — bounds set with margin over the design rotor's computed operating
envelope (49.4k–854k), not chosen for round numbers; the full derivation is
`reynolds_envelope` in `config/polars_sg6043.yaml`.

Generated by `python -m xfoil.build_polar_cache sg6043` (run from `src/`);
validated by `python -m validation.validate_polars sg6043` (3/5 — see
*Validation* below for what the two failing checks mean here, which is
different from why S809 fails one).

**These are built at `Ncrit = 9`, XFOIL's own clean-tunnel default** — the
opposite calibration story from S809's `Ncrit = 5`. S809's value was chosen to
model *atmospheric* turbulence for a thick, stall-prone section; the UIUC
Low-Speed Airfoil Tests facility this cache is checked against *is* a clean
low-turbulence tunnel, so matching the measurement environment is the
theoretically expected choice here, and it is what the n_crit sensitivity
study (`results/ncrit_sensitivity/`) selected on
its own merits. The two calibrations are not supposed to agree, and they
don't.

## File format, and what the fifth column means

Each CSV is `alpha,cl,cd,cm,source` and spans **−180 … 180°** on a 0.5° grid,
721 rows per file, the same convention as the S809 table:

| code | label | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `xfoil` | converged in the original sweep |
| 1 | `gap_retry` | converged in the finer-step retry that closed a hole |
| 2 | `local_fit` | **not converged anywhere** — quadratic through the nearest converged points |
| 3 | `viterna` | post-stall extrapolation, outside the −8…18° converged band |

Definition in `src/polars/cache_format.py`; `load_xfoil_band()` returns the
measured rows only, which is what `validate_polars.py` and
`compare_sg6043_uiuc.py` both read.

## Build history and the Re=60k/80k hang

The main sweep (`n_iter=200, timeout=120`, rather than S809's 400/900) completed clean for all 13
Reynolds numbers — no crash, no run lost. The Re=60k negative-alpha-leg
failure flagged as a real risk going into this session (XFOIL hanging past
its stall peak, discarding the bidirectional sweep's negative leg entirely)
**did not materialise at that scale**: Re=60k needed 12 gap-retry points
during the main build and 10 more local-fit points afterward (§ below) — a
real but bounded gap, not the large swath that was anticipated.

## Viterna extension, and the stitch

Outside −8…18° the files carry the classical Viterna extrapolation from
`src/polars/viterna.py`, with `CD_MAX = 1.3206` **derived** for this cache
from `1.11 + 0.018·AR` at the design rotor's own aspect ratio (11.7) —
*not* S809's QBlade-fitted 1.8, which has no bearing on a 2 m blade
(`config/polars_sg6043.yaml`, *extrapolation* section).

**Value continuity at the upper stitch is exact** — worst |jump| across all
13 Reynolds rows is 2.22e-16 (floating-point rounding). The slope is not
continuous at either stitch, and — as for S809 — the size of the kink is
recorded rather than assumed, since the C¹ interpolant has to make an
explicit choice about it (`python -m validation.check_stitch_continuity
sg6043`, full JSON in `results/polar_cache/sg6043_stitch_continuity.json`).

## Validation against experimental data (UIUC LSAT Vol. 3)

`python -m validation.compare_sg6043_uiuc` (from `src/`) compares the
finished, gap-closed cache's XFOIL-converged band against
`data/sg6043_uiuc_lsat/` at the six Reynolds numbers the two share. This is a
genuine measured-vs-computed check — S809 has no equivalent independent
reference at its own build settings, so this is the stronger of the two
caches' validations.

| Re | Cl RMSE | Cd RMSE | Cl points (xfoil/gap_retry/local_fit) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100,000 | 0.1442 | 0.00811 | 39 / 12 / 2 |
| 150,000 | 0.1488 | 0.00371 | 42 / 9 / 2 |
| 200,000 | 0.1135 | 0.00376 | 43 / 2 / 8 |
| 300,000 | 0.0907 | 0.01298 | 45 / 0 / 8 |
| 400,000 | 0.0272 | 0.00941 | 46 / 0 / 7 |
| 500,000 | 0.0240 | 0.01338 | 46 / 0 / 3 |
| **mean** | **0.0914** | **0.00856** | |

Cl agreement improves monotonically with Re — 0.144 at the cache floor's
nearest measured point down to 0.024 at 500k — which tracks the n_crit study's
own finding that low-Re XFOIL is the least trustworthy regime here, not a
build defect. Cd agreement is small and roughly flat across the range
(0.004–0.013), i.e. an order of magnitude below Cl error as expected for the
much smaller quantity. Provenance is reported alongside every RMSE so a low
number achieved partly across filled points is visible rather than hidden
inside one figure. Full JSON and per-Reynolds comparison plots (cache vs.
UIUC, points colour-coded by provenance): `results/sg6043_uiuc_validation/`.

## Validation (`validate_polars.py`, physical plausibility): 3/5

Checks 1 (grid completeness), 3 (pre-stall monotonicity) and 4 (drag sanity)
pass. Checks 2 (lift-slope vs. thin-airfoil theory) and 5 (Reynolds-trend
monotonicity) fail — and, unlike S809's one failing check, this is not noise
in a flat quantity. It is a specific, located, physically-explained effect:

**A real laminar-separation-bubble burst, not a data artifact.** At Re=40,000
the cache jumps from Cl=1.1137 at α=8.5° to Cl=1.4287 at α=9.0° — a slope of
0.63/deg against a thin-airfoil expectation of 0.11/deg — and **every point on
both sides of the jump is `source=xfoil`**, not a fill. This is the classic
signature of a bubble suddenly bursting and the flow reattaching before true
stall, well documented for thin sections at low Re; it is what triggers
Check 2's slope-bound violation at 40k, 60k, 80k, 100k and 200k (each less
severe than the last, consistent with the bubble weakening as Re rises).

**Check 5's Cl_max non-monotonicity is the same phenomenon, aggregated**: Cl_max
dips from 1.673 (100k) to 1.646 (300k) before rising again to 1.843 (1.0M).
The 300k value (1.6456) sits within 0.008 of the n_crit sensitivity study's
own free-transition sanity check against the UIUC lift curve (1.654) — i.e.
this is the same calibrated behaviour the n_crit study already validated at
that Reynolds number, not a new discrepancy introduced by the full build.
Cl(0) varying by 0.638 across the range (0.144 at 40k to 0.782 at 1.0M) is
the same low-Re bubble effect reducing the section's effective camber, and is
corroborated by the UIUC comparison table above showing exactly this Reynolds
band as the one with the largest measured-vs-computed Cl error.

This is recorded, not tuned away. Nothing in
`n_crit`, `cd_max`, or the alpha range was adjusted to make Check 2/5 pass —
doing so would fit the calibration to an internal-consistency test instead of
the UIUC measurements it is actually checked against above.

## Known gaps and limitations carried forward

- **Re = 40k–80k have no experimental support.** UIUC's floor is 100k; these
  three curves are XFOIL alone, in the regime flagged as least trustworthy.
  Named in `config/polars_sg6043.yaml`'s `low_re_caveat`; unchanged by this
  build.
- The 79 interior holes above are all outside the design rotor's rated-operation
  Reynolds band (~150k–450k), but they are inside the low-Re region several
  wind-speed bins actually visit below rated power — see
  `reynolds_envelope.cases.below_rated` in the config.
- The C¹ interpolant was verified against S809 and hasn't been re-verified
  specifically against this table's own stitch kinks or laminar-bubble jump;
  that would be follow-up work.
